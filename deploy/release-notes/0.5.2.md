## A device is reached the moment it arrives

0.5.1 tried a device as soon as a packet was for it. When the other device was not there yet, the
attempt kept trying for fifteen seconds, but with gaps of up to eight seconds, so a device that came
back in the middle could wait seconds for the next try. In this version:

- **With the updated relay, a device that comes back is reached a round trip later.** The relay
  remembers, for fifteen seconds, who tried to reach a device that was not connected, and tells
  them when it connects; their attempt is sent again at once. Nobody else hears of it, and nothing
  is sent while devices rest.
- **With a relay not yet updated, it is reached within two seconds.** An attempt is sent again every
  two seconds for fifteen seconds (was after 1, 3 and 7 seconds).
- **A device that loses its link to the relay is back within about 22 seconds** (was up to a
  minute and a half). A packet to send brings the next try forward. A relay that refuses or does not
  answer is still asked at most once a minute.
- **What the relay remembers for this is bounded.** At most 64 recipients per sender and 200,000
  over all senders (about 100 MB at the worst), each forgotten fifteen seconds after the last
  attempt. Both are set in the relay's `[limits]` as `asked_per_sender` and `asked_total`. The same
  bounds now cover the relay's "not connected" notices, which before had no limit of their own.

## A power cut no longer counts against the TPM

On Linux, the daemon checked at start that the TPM could sign with a key that counted against its
lockout. When the power went before the daemon stopped, the TPM counted the next start as a wrong
guess; after a few such cuts it locked itself, and the daemon then reported this machine's key as
lost, and every network as to be joined again.

- **The check now uses a key that does not count,** so power cuts cost nothing.
- **A TPM that cannot be used now is no longer taken for a key that is gone.** The daemon says the
  TPM cannot be used, removes nothing, and asks to be started again once it answers. Only a TPM that
  was cleared, or hardware that changed, means joining each network again.

Nothing changes between devices: 0.5.2, 0.5.1 and 0.5.0 devices work together, with the old relay
or the new one; a device before 0.5.2 ignores the relay's arrival notice. Update the relay to get
the arrival notice, and every device to get the whole of it. Rebuild the sketch with this version's
Arduino library.
